## Methods

### Studies
Published maize RNA-seq studies that expose plants to low temperature (chilling, freezing, cold during germination, growth in a cool environment, or recovery after cold), with raw reads in public repositories (NCBI SRA/GEO, ENA, CNCB GSA). Accessions were resolved from papers and repository records, and every run was mapped to a biological sample with its genotype, tissue, temperature (day and night), control temperature, phase, time and other factors. Sample labels were checked against the cold response of marker genes; one deposit whose condition labels were swapped was corrected. The app shows the studies used in the analysis: those whose data passed quantification and library QC and that give at least one cold contrast.

### Quantification
FASTQ files were downloaded on NC State's Hazel cluster with MD5 checks, trimmed with fastp and quantified with salmon (selective alignment, decoy-aware index) against B73 RefGen_v5 gene models Zm00001eb.1 using nf-core/rnaseq 3.27.0, then summarised to genes with tximport. QuantSeq 3′ tag libraries (Xue2021) had poly(A) tails and the first 12 nt trimmed and were quantified without length correction. Other genotypes are mapped to B73.

### Library QC (strict)
A library is left out if it has fewer than 5 M mapped reads (2 M for 3′ tags and for one study of shallow libraries), maps below 60% or more than 10 points below other libraries of the same study, tissue and genotype, has a different library type from the rest of its study, or disagrees with its replicates while they agree with each other (when only two replicates disagree, both go). A root, leaf or shoot library is also left out when it carries tissue from the other organ: marker genes of the other tissue (80 photosynthesis genes for roots, 78 root-specific genes for leaves and shoots, from the MaizExpressN studies that sequenced both) make up at least three times the share of its mRNA seen in the study's other libraries of that tissue and in its own replicates, at least 0.5 percentage points more, with at least a fifth of those markers raised three-fold.

Two refinements for cold data. Forward- and reverse-stranded libraries count as the same prep when they differ only in mate order. And the relative mapping rule is not applied when a whole replicate group maps low: long cold lowers mapping in every replicate because more reads fall outside annotated transcripts (it reverses on recovery), which is a response to the treatment, not a failed library. Contrasts that lose replication become display-only.

### Contrasts, classes and sign
Within each study, every cold group is compared with a control of the same genotype, tissue and other factors: the control sampled at the same time if there is one, otherwise the study's single control, otherwise the time-0 sample (marked “not time-matched”). log2FC = log2(cold / control), so positive means higher under cold; the sidebar toggle flips the sign. Classes:

- **Chilling**: cold above 0 °C (including roots in iced water at 0 °C) against the control temperature.
- **Freezing**: below 0 °C.
- **Recovery**: back at the control temperature after a cold step, against the control (the filter temperature is that of the cold step).
- **Germination**: cold during imbibition, germination or emergence (seeds, embryos, radicles and seedlings germinated in the cold).
- **Chronic**: plants grown from sowing in a cool chamber against a warm one, compared at the same developmental stage.

“Acute cold” pools chilling and freezing. Germination, chronic and recovery designs are kept apart because they ask different questions. Controls vary between studies (15 to 32 °C) and so does the cold step (−1 to 22 °C); both are listed with every contrast, and the temperature and time filters apply to them. Contrasts that change something besides temperature (cold combined with soil moisture or with a move between growth rooms), cold applied to one organ only, and second comparisons against a time-0 control are shown but not pooled.

Each study is analysed with DESeq2 (`~ 0 + group`), one Wald test per contrast with BH-adjusted p within the contrast. Contrasts whose replicates were independent repeats (separate plantings or sequencing batches) are fitted with the repeat as a blocking factor. In the 25-inbred diversity panel, each inbred has one cold and one control library, so the panel contrast compares cold with control paired by inbred (`~ genotype + temperature`). Groups with a single library are left out of the model fit (they add no information on variance); their contrasts show the log2 ratio of mean normalised counts without SE and are never pooled. Shrunken (ashr) estimates are stored for display; unshrunken log2FC and SE are used for pooling.

A fold change carried by a single library is not tested. In a replicated contrast, a gene is marked “one library” when one library holds more than 90% of its normalised counts across the contrast's libraries, or when it reaches padj < 0.05 but its higher condition, without its top library, is no higher than the lower condition. As with DESeq2's Cook's outliers, such genes get no p-value; their log2FC is still shown (× in the plots) but is not pooled.

### Pooling
Counts are never pooled across studies, only effect sizes. Contrasts that share study, tissue group and class are first combined by inverse variance, so one study counts once however many genotypes, times or temperatures it has; studies are then combined with DerSimonian–Laird random effects (pooled log2FC, 95% CI, p, τ², I²). Estimates without a p-value (one library, or a DESeq2 Cook's outlier) are left out. Gene lookup and Gene list pool on the fly under the current filters; Rank genes uses precomputed tables (all genotypes or B73 only).

### Positive controls
Eight maize DREB1/CBF genes are used as positive controls: ereb65, ereb36, dbp4, ereb23, ereb16, ereb105, ereb79 and dbf3. Seven have the CBF-family member DDF2/DREB1F as their closest Arabidopsis protein (Ensembl lists no one-to-one maize orthologue of CBF1–3); ereb23 has no Ensembl match, but its closest protein elsewhere is a DREB1A and it lies next to ereb36 in a cluster of DREB1 genes. They were chosen from the annotation and then checked against the data: in leaf, shoot and seedling contrasts at 10 °C or below, each is up in most contrasts (median log2FC +4 to +8), while roots and chronic cool growth do not induce them. Four dehydrins (dhn2, dhn3, dhn13, dhn15) respond more weakly; the three ICE1 homologues (SCREAM-like bHLH) are regulated after translation and change little. The gene card marks these genes, the Gene list page starts with them, and the Studies page gives, for each replicated contrast, how many of the eight are up.

### Co-expression
Two genes are compared in two ways. **Across samples**: within each study and tissue group, the Pearson correlation of log2(TPM + 1) over its libraries (a library missing from the TPM tables counts as 0). With “within conditions”, each condition group’s mean is removed first, which gives the partial correlation given the condition, so a shared response to the cold treatment does not count. Fisher-z standard errors are 1/√(n − 3) across all libraries and 1/√(n − g − 2) within conditions (g condition groups). Units where either gene has a mean TPM below the threshold, or with fewer than three residual degrees of freedom, are left out. Studies are pooled with DerSimonian–Laird random effects on Fisher z, by tissue group and overall. **Across contrasts**: each point is a contrast where both genes were tested, with its two log2FC values; contrasts from one study share libraries and controls, so the Pearson and Spearman correlations there are descriptive. The sidebar’s tissue, tolerance, growth-system, B73-only and excluded-study filters apply to the sample view; all filters apply to the contrast view.

**Strongest partners** of gene A are precomputed for every gene with the same across-samples statistic (all included libraries, minimum mean TPM 1, no sidebar filters), in three scopes: all study × tissue units, root units and leaf units, each across all libraries and within conditions. A gene has a list in a scope when its mean TPM is at least 1 in at least half of the scope's units; a pair is ranked only when at least half of the units express both genes, and pairs are ranked by pooled r (top 25 in each direction). Very long transcripts tend to rise and fall together with library-to-library differences in RNA integrity, and neighbouring paralogs can share reads; partners within 100 kb are marked “nearby”.

### Gene annotation
MaizeGDB Zm00001eb.1 symbols, names, gene products and GO terms; v3/v4 cross-references from MaizeGDB; Arabidopsis orthologs and gene features from Ensembl Plants. Descriptions marked “predicted” come from MaizeGDB's EnTAP annotation.

The gene card also shows data from MaizeMine, MaizeGDB's data warehouse (release and download date in the app data): metabolic pathways (MaizeGDB E2P2 pathways and Plant Reactome); GWAS hits within 10 kb of the gene, from the GWAS Atlas and the Wallace et al. 2014 NAM GWAS, listed by trait with the nearest hit first; protein-changing variants from the Ensembl Plants variant set (distinct stop-gained, frameshift, splice-site, start-lost and stop-lost variants, and missense variants with the number SIFT predicts deleterious, low-confidence calls included); and the gene's MaizeGDB pan-gene group, with the B73 and NAM founder genomes that have a copy. A GWAS hit near a gene is not evidence that the gene causes the trait.

The pan-genome strip shows, for the B73 and 25 NAM founder genomes in order, whether the gene's pan-gene group has a copy there. **Literature** counts gene-specific papers from NCBI gene2pubmed: papers NCBI links to the gene, keeping only those linked to at most 10 maize genes, so genome-scale papers are left out. NCBI's links are incomplete, so the count is a lower bound. **Baseline expression** is the MaizeGDB B73 expression atlas from MaizeMine: mean FPKM per tissue and developmental stage under standard growth conditions, with no cold treatment. It shows where a gene is normally expressed, not how it responds to cold.

### Caveats
- Cold and control temperatures differ between studies; mild cold (15 °C and above) and cool controls (below 20 °C) give smaller effects. Filter by temperature to compare like with like.
- Time-0 controls confound cold with time of day and plant age; chronic designs compare plants of the same stage but different age.
- Non-B73 genotypes are mapped to B73, which biases expression for divergent genes. Use “B73 only” to check.
- Effects from 3′ tag-seq, single root zones or combined treatments behave differently from standard whole-organ mRNA-seq.
